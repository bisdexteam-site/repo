Vendor: FactSet Research Systems, Inc.
Best known for: FactSet has been a leading provider of accurate financial information since 1978. Similar to[ Bloomberg](https://sites.google.com/view/bisdex/sources-reviews/bloomberg-terminal) and[ Capital IQ](https://sites.google.com/view/bisdex/sources-reviews/capital-iq), FactSet is considered a major forecast data provider in the financial sector. With notable partnerships like[ Crunchbase](https://sites.google.com/view/bisdex/sources-reviews/crunchbase) for private company intelligence and Insider Intelligence for advertising spending, FactSet combines data and text from multiple information providers (800+ sources) into a single online source of information, news, and analytics. 

FactSet powers the investment community, so exposure to FactSet is helpful for students to learn how to:

* Monitor trends within companies, economies, and global markets
* Sift through business news summaries and analyst commentary with StreetAccount
* Benchmark company, industry, ESG, and investors’ performance data
* Personalize dashboards for screening


As a differentiator in the financial information marketplace, FactSet news and document search offer advanced search options across key document types, such as company/peer/sector transcripts, investor slides, filings, and internal company research.   
In terms of screening capabilities, watchlists, industry sector news, and other charting features, FactSet is probably most similar to LSEG Workspace and S&P Capital IQ Pro. All of these providers offer an abundance of options for customizing dashboards and conducting analysis on deep company and industry-related content.
Compared to BvD Osiris, the FactSet database includes information on the most public companies in Europe.
Among international company databases, Osiris and FactSet often include the largest number of observations with accounting items.



Reviews



Researchers screening across hundreds or thousands of companies may find the interface (UX) tedious or even unusable for their purposes. It’s sometimes unclear which data is available via the FactSet Web and Workstation, and which data require additional purchases via WRDS (what FactSet refers to as their data feeds). For example, current supply chain relationships are available via the FactSet Web and Workstation, but historical supply chain partnerships are not. So although some history is available in FactSet for academia, much of the individual content items and especially their historical data, require additional purchases. FactSet data and analysis is more likely to include larger public companies in developed markets, rather than private companies in developing markets. FactSet entitlements do not default to include any equity research.
