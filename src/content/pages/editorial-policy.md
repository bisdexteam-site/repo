## Purpose and scope

BISdex publishes reviews of commercial business databases written by librarians who use them. This policy explains what we accept, how submissions are moderated, and how we keep reviews fair and trustworthy. It applies to every review submitted through the BISdex review form.

## Core principles

Four commitments guide every editorial decision.

- **Authenticity.** Reviews come from real information professionals and librarians describing their own hands-on use of a resource.
- **Accuracy.** Descriptive information about a resource, including content, features, and access notes, should be correct and verifiable.
- **Integrity.** We do not allow fake reviews, coordinated campaigns, or misleading claims.
- **Objectivity.** Reviews are personal assessments. They reflect each reviewer's context and judgment. Opinions in a review belong to its author, not to BISdex, the Business Information Sources Committee, BRASS, RUSA, or ALA.

## Who may submit and what we expect

Only librarians or information professionals who have used a resource may review it. The following may not submit reviews, because each has a conflict of interest: vendors; their current and former employees; employees of competing vendors; and anyone paid or rewarded by a vendor.

A review must:

- Describe your own experience with the resource, honestly and in good faith
- Avoid confidential information, such as pricing or terms covered by a nondisclosure clause
- Disclose any relationship with the vendor that a reader would want to know about

We encourage specific examples, such as a research task the resource handled well or poorly. A review will not be rejected only because it is brief or general.

## Use of generative AI

You may use generative AI tools to help organize or polish a review, as long as the experience and opinions are your own.

- Tell us on the submission form if you used an AI tool and how. You do not need to disclose AI used only for copyediting.
- Revise any AI-assisted text so it reflects what you actually observed, in your own words.
- AI-assisted reviews meet the same standards as every other review.

Every review is held to the standards of research integrity expected of librarians. Reviews written mainly by AI, or describing experiences the reviewer did not have, will be rejected.

## How reviews are moderated

Every review is read by a BISdex moderator before it is published. Nothing goes live automatically.

1. **Submission.** A reviewer completes the Electronic Resource Review Form, either from the "Leave a review" button on a source page or for a resource not yet listed.
2. **Eligibility check.** A moderator confirms the submission is complete, concerns a business information resource, and appears to come from a real user with first-hand experience.
3. **Content review.** The moderator checks the review against this policy, looking for false statements of fact, personal attacks, confidential terms, and undisclosed conflicts of interest.
4. **Decision.** The moderator approves the review, returns it to the reviewer for revision, or declines it.
5. **Publication.** Editors post approved reviews to the resource's Reviews section.

Reviews marked "Verified by BISdex editors" have been through this process: editors verify the reviewer's identity, check for conflicts of interest, and evaluate the review against this policy.

Before a review is published, moderators may correct spelling and formatting. They do not change the reviewer's wording, meaning, or assessment, and they do not edit a review once it is published.

## Relevancy ratings

Reviewers may rate how relevant a resource is to three groups of users. Ratings are anecdotal: they reflect one reviewer's experience at their own institution, not a measurement or a BISdex assessment.

- **Researchers:** faculty, doctoral students, and others doing scholarly research
- **Teaching / Students:** instruction, coursework, and student assignments
- **Commercial / Industry / Entrepreneurs:** practitioners, business owners, and job seekers

Each group is rated on a three-point scale:

- **High:** a go-to resource for this group's typical needs
- **Average:** useful for some of this group's needs, usually alongside other resources
- **Low:** rarely the best fit for this group

## Reviewer privacy

Published reviews do not show the reviewer's name or institution. Instead, each review shows the reviewer's role, institution type, experience, and relationship to the resource.

Moderators see the reviewer's contact details only so they can follow up. BISdex does not share those details with vendors or the public.

## Fair treatment of reviews

We apply the same standards to every review, whether it praises a resource or criticizes it.

- We never ask for positive reviews only.
- We do not hide, delay, or bury critical reviews.
- Vendors cannot pay for or request removal of specific reviews.
- A review is declined only for failing this policy, never for its tone or conclusion.
- BISdex does not offer payment, gifts, or any other incentive for reviews.

Vendors may respond to a review by emailing BISdex. Editors read each response and decide whether to publish it alongside the review.

## Declined and removed reviews

If a review is declined or later removed, we will tell the reviewer why by email. Reviewers may revise and resubmit.

We may remove a published review that we learn breaks this policy, for example one that turns out to be fake or to contain a false statement of fact.

## Updating a review

Because published reviews are anonymous, reviewers cannot edit or withdraw them directly. A reviewer who wants to update a review may submit a new one through the review form. Editors will decide whether it replaces the earlier review or appears as an additional review.

## Accessibility grades

Each source page shows a BISdex WCAG grade: a letter grade for how well the resource meets the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG). Grades belong to the source information that BISdex editors maintain. They are never part of a review, and reviewers do not assign them.

The editorial board assigns each grade from the WCAG Criteria Compliance score in the resource's Library Accessibility Alliance (LAA) evaluation, using the scale below. The source page links to that evaluation and gives its year.

## Corrections and concerns

Anyone, including a vendor, may report a factual error. Moderators will review the report and respond. Vendors may request corrections to a source page's source information, such as its Description and Content sections, by email.
